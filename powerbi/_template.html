Britain’s grid is twice as clean at lunchtime as at teatime

Ninety days of half-hourly carbon intensity, pulled from the National Energy System Operator’s open API, validated, loaded into a star schema and modelled in Power BI. This page is a static preview of the four report pages.

The shape of a British day

Average actual intensity for each of the 48 half-hour slots, local time

The grid has a rhythm, and it is not the one most people assume. Solar and lower demand pull the middle of the day down; the evening peak, when everyone gets home and gas plant is called on, pushes it to its daily worst. Overnight sits in between.

What to do with this. Move discretionary electricity use out of 17:00–21:00 and into 11:00–14:00. On these ninety days that cuts the carbon intensity of whatever is moved by roughly 45%, without reducing how much is used.

For anything reported or billed, use the settled actual rather than the forecast, and wait for settlement before publishing a figure.

Where the forecast struggles

Daily mean absolute error across the window

Spikes line up with rapid changes in wind output, which the forecast reacts to late

Verdict. Trust it for choosing a time of day. The error is small relative to the 70-gram swing between midday and teatime, so the ranking of hours is reliable even when the level is a little off.

Do not trust it to the gram. There is a consistent optimistic bias, and the worst half-hour in the window was out by 165.

What the pipeline checked before loading anything

Nine checks run on every execution and are written to the warehouse, not just to a log. A check marked error stops the run and nothing is loaded. A check marked warning is recorded and the run continues, because an unsettled actual is a property of the source rather than a defect.

Two days are off the line, for two different reasons. 25 October is the autumn clock change, when the local day genuinely holds 50 half-hours. 8 August holds 46 because the requested window opens at midnight UTC while the clocks were on BST, so the first two local half-hours fall outside it.
